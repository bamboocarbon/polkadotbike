Every stage and climb of the La Vuelta a España 2026

The La Vuelta a España 2026 runs from Sat 22 August (Monaco) to Sun 13 September (Granada) — 21 stages, 6 of them mountain stages, with 51 categorised climbs including 4 especial-category ascents. The highest point of the race is Port d'Envalira at 2,409 m. Every stage and climb is listed below with its length, average gradient and summit altitude — follow any climb's link to open it in the Climb Planner and work out the gearing you'd need to ride it yourself.

Stage 1 · Sat 22 August · Monaco → Monaco

Individual time trial · 9 km · 90 m of climbing

Opening time trial through the Principality's streets — technical, with the first race leader decided by seconds.

Stage 2 · Sun 23 August · Monaco → Manosque

Hilly stage · 215 km · 3,060 m of climbing

Longest stage of the race. Technical start through Nice before Provence's rolling lavender country; two Cat3 climbs (Alto Châteauneuf-Grasse, Col de Saint Andrieu) should suit the fast finishers.

  • Cat3Alto Châteauneuf-Grasse — 7.4 km at 3%, summit 410 m, 170.6 km from the finish. Gradual foothill climb above the Riviera.
  • Cat3Col de Saint Andrieu — 5.86 km at 4.4%, summit 508 m, 109 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot's GPS route data (confirmed by Robin as climb #6 on the official Komoot stage map).

Stage 3 · Mon 24 August · Gruissan → Font Romeu

Medium mountain stage · 166 km · 2,660 m of climbing

Flat start gradually rising into the Pyrénées Orientales. Col de Mont-Louis (Cat1) sets up the first summit finish of the race at Font Romeu (Cat2), 1,937m.

  • Cat1Col de Mont-Louis — 8.6 km at 5.75%, summit 1,570 m, 15.3 km from the finish. Steady mid-mountain grind via Fontpédrouse; sets up the descent into Font Romeu.
  • Cat2Font Romeu — 9.87 km at 4.5%, summit 1,937 m, stage finish. First summit finish of the race. Short final rise off the Mont-Louis descent — matches organisers' own description of the last km easing to around 4.5%. Length/gradient from Komoot's GPS route data (best-guess match by position — not independently cross-checked against another source).

Stage 4 · Tue 25 August · Andorra la Vella → Andorra la Vella

Mountain stage · 104 km · 2,890 m of climbing

Short but brutal loop entirely within Andorra: Port d'Envalira (Cat1, the highest paved pass in the Pyrenees), Collada de Beixalís (Cat1) and Coll d'Ordino (Cat1) before a short finishing ramp at Alto de La Comella (Cat3).

  • Cat1Port d'Envalira — 25.3 km at 5.1%, summit 2,409 m, 78.7 km from the finish. The highest paved pass in the Pyrenees. Long and steady from Andorra la Vella via El Tarter — altitude is the real difficulty, not the gradient.
  • Cat1Collada de Beixalís — 6.7 km at 8.3%, summit 1,803 m, 44.6 km from the finish. Irregular, narrow road via Encamp with hairpins and ramps over 20% — the toughest climb of the day pound for pound.
  • Cat1Coll d'Ordino — 9.8 km at 7.1%, summit 1,989 m, 25.2 km from the finish. Steady lower slopes via Ordino, harder in the closing kilometres. Steepest section 10.7%.
  • Cat3Alto de La Comella — 4.1 km at 5.9%, summit 1,347 m, 3.9 km from the finish. Short finishing ramp back into Andorra la Vella. Length/gradient from Komoot's route elevation data (unofficial, not yet cross-checked against another source).

Stage 5 · Wed 26 August · Falset. Costa Daurada → Roquetes. Terres de l'Ebre

Hilly stage · 171 km · 1,690 m of climbing

Alto de Paüls (Cat3) is the only real difficulty; likely a sprint finish in the Terres de l'Ebre.

  • Cat3Alto de Paüls — 4.1 km at 6%, summit 381 m, 23.4 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot.

Stage 6 · Thu 27 August · Alcossebre → Castelló

Medium mountain stage · 176 km · 3,080 m of climbing

Puerto de la Serratella and Alto del Desierto de las Palmas (both Cat2) before a dirt-road finale up Puerto El Bartolo (Cat1, 3km unpaved) and a fast descent into Castelló.

  • Cat2Puerto de la Serratella — 19 km at 3.3%, summit 799 m, 135.4 km from the finish. Long, gradual ascent — categorised more for cumulative altitude gain than steepness.
  • Cat3Coll de la Bandereta — 4.7 km at 6.7%, summit 785 m, 109.8 km from the finish. Climbed from the west (Rosildos side) before descending to Benlloch, confirmed against the route map. Max gradient 9.6%.
  • Cat2Alto del Desierto de las Palmas — 9.5 km at 4.3%, summit 418 m, 47.5 km from the finish. Climbed from Benicàssim on the coast.
  • Cat1Puerto El Bartolo — 11.7 km at 6%, summit 713 m, 19.1 km from the finish. First-ever dirt-road finale for La Vuelta — the final ~3.5km run on gravel/sterrato before a fast paved descent into Castelló. Steepest ramp 17.2%.

Stage 7 · Fri 28 August · Vall d'Alba → Aramón Valdelinares

Mountain stage · 149 km · 3,750 m of climbing

Second summit finish of the race, climbing into Teruel's Sierra de Gúdar via Puerto El Remolcador and Puerto de San Rafael (both Cat2) before the ski-station finish at Valdelinares.

  • Cat2Puerto El Remolcador — 10.3 km at 4.2%, summit 982 m, 96.4 km from the finish.
  • Cat3Alto de Zucaina — 5.41 km at 5%, summit 814 m, 82.1 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot.
  • Cat3Puerto Fuente de Rubielos — 11.4 km at 3.3%, summit 1,002 m, 43.6 km from the finish. Gradual, sustained ascent.
  • Cat2Puerto de San Rafael — 14.2 km at 3.7%, summit 1,558 m, 12.3 km from the finish. Long false-flat approach before the road continues on to the Valdelinares finish.
  • Cat1Aramón Valdelinares — 9.08 km at 4.6%, summit 1,961 m, stage finish. Summit finish at the Sierra de Gúdar ski station, climbed via Puerto de San Rafael from Mora de Rubielos. Length/gradient from Komoot's GPS route data — the last (and only major) climb on this stage, so a confident match for the actual finish ascent, replacing an earlier figure that was mistakenly for the Alcalá de la Selva side.

Stage 8 · Sat 29 August · Puçol → Xeraco

Flat stage · 168 km · 1,270 m of climbing

Last chance for the sprinters in week one; Puerto de Barx (Cat2) crests just before the finish in Xeraco.

  • Cat2Puerto de Barx — 7.42 km at 4%, summit 317 m, 22.2 km from the finish. Crests just before the finish in Xeraco. Length/gradient from Komoot.

Stage 9 · Sun 30 August · La Vila Joiosa / Villajoyosa → Alto de Aitana. Costa Blanca

Mountain stage · 187 km · 4,940 m of climbing

Brutal way to close the first week — six categorised climbs and nearly 5,000m of climbing, including Puerto de El Miserat (Cat1), before the legendary Alto de Aitana summit finish above the Costa Blanca.

  • Cat2Puerto de Tárbena — 12.1 km at 3%, summit 594 m, 150.3 km from the finish. Gradual, early climb of the day.
  • Cat1Puerto de El Miserat — 18 km at 2.6%, summit 634 m, 113 km from the finish. Long and gradual rather than steep — the Cat1 reflects length and cumulative altitude gain.
  • Cat3Puerto de Tollos — 4.24 km at 5.6%, summit 829 m, 96.2 km from the finish. Climbed from the north — length/gradient from Komoot, cross-checked against CyclingCols' matching "North, from CV-712/CV-713" profile (4.8km/4.5%).
  • Cat2Puerto de Tudons — 4.99 km at 6%, summit 1,027 m, 65.1 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot, the specific final segment up to the summit (not the much longer climb-from-the-stage-start figure CyclingCols lists).
  • Cat3Puerto de Benifallim — 10.5 km at 3%, summit 1,013 m, 46.8 km from the finish. Gradual approach into the closing kilometres before Aitana.
  • ESPAlto de Aitana — 21.5 km at 5.8%, summit 1,545 m, stage finish. One of Spain's hardest summit finishes, used by La Vuelta in 2015, 2019 and 2021. Long, irregular grind with a max gradient around 9.2%.

Stage 10 · Tue 1 September · Alcaraz → Elche de la Sierra

Hilly stage · 184 km · 2,720 m of climbing

Winding stage through Albacete province after the first rest day; three Cat3 climbs (Puerto del Peralejo, Puerto de Aýna, Puerto de Socovos) could suit a breakaway.

  • Cat3Puerto del Peralejo — 13.5 km at 1.5%, summit 1,132 m, 136.4 km from the finish. Very gradual climb from Fábricas de Riópar — length/gradient from CyclingCols, confirmed as the correct approach side by matching the stage's actual direction of travel. Max gradient 9.2%.
  • Cat3Puerto de Aýna — 7 km at 3.7%, summit 950 m, 97.7 km from the finish.
  • Cat3Puerto de Socovos — 3.31 km at 5%, summit 782 m, 20.7 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot, the final segment leading to the summit. A separate, earlier rise (1.74km at 6%) also appears before it on the same approach.

Stage 11 · Wed 2 September · Cartagena → Lorca

Flat stage · 156 km · 1,310 m of climbing

Another sprint opportunity, but Alto del Morrón de Totana (Cat3) crests just 30km from the finish.

  • Cat3Alto del Morrón de Totana — 11.6 km at 3.3%, summit 624 m, 33.5 km from the finish. Crests around 30km from the finish in Lorca.

Stage 12 · Thu 3 September · Vera → Calar Alto

Mountain stage · 166 km · 4,530 m of climbing

Andalucía's toughest welcome: the traditional Velefique–Calar Alto double, climbing to Europe's largest astronomical observatory at over 2,100m.

  • Cat3Alto del Cabecico — 4.28 km at 4%, summit 471 m, 149.9 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot.
  • Cat3Alto Cóbdar — 5.33 km at 5%, summit 668 m, 108.4 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot.
  • Cat2Collado García — 4.15 km at 4.8%, summit 1,239 m, 79.3 km from the finish. Length/gradient from Komoot, combining two consecutive detected segments (1.53km + 2.62km, both ~5%) into one continuous climb.
  • Cat1Alto de Velefique — 20.1 km at 5.7%, summit 1,789 m, 30.4 km from the finish. Climbed from Velefique town, cresting before a descent toward Bacares and the final push to Calar Alto. Length/gradient from CyclingCols' "South, from Velefique" profile — an exact match for this stage's approach town. Max gradient 11.0%.
  • ESPCalar Alto — 18.19 km at 5.15%, summit 2,153 m, stage finish. Summit finish at Europe's largest astronomical observatory, over 2,100m — one of the hardest climbs in the south of Spain. Length/gradient calculated from Komoot's elevation chart between the Bacares altitude/distance markers (1,196m to the 2,134m summit) — a strong match to the roadbook's own Bacares altitude of 1,197m.

Stage 13 · Fri 4 September · Almuñécar → Loja

Medium mountain stage · 193 km · 3,410 m of climbing

Winding stage that front-loads its difficulty — Venta de la Cebada (Cat1) and Alto del Legionario (Cat2) come in the first half, with a flatter run to Loja.

  • Cat1Venta de la Cebada — 7.4 km at 6.7%, summit 819 m, 165 km from the finish.
  • Cat2Alto del Legionario — 22 km at 2.6%, summit 1,347 m, 109 km from the finish. Long, gradual ascent via Padul.
  • Cat2Puerto de Granada — 13.5 km at 3.3%, summit 1,034 m, 37 km from the finish.

Stage 14 · Sat 5 September · Jaén → Sierra de la Pandera

Mountain stage · 152 km · 4,170 m of climbing

One of La Vuelta's most decisive summit finishes since 2002 — Puerto de Los Villares and Puerto de Locubín (both Cat2) set up the final ramps to Sierra de la Pandera.

  • Cat2Puerto de Los Villares — 11.4 km at 5.2%, summit 1,180 m, 60.6 km from the finish.
  • Cat2Puerto de Locubín — 10.1 km at 4.5%, summit 1,085 m, 20.4 km from the finish. Leads into the final push to Sierra de la Pandera.
  • ESPSierra de la Pandera — 8.4 km at 7.8%, summit 1,797 m, stage finish. One of La Vuelta's classic brutal summit finishes since 2002, with several sectors over 15% — including inside the final kilometre, where it touches 18%.

Stage 15 · Sun 6 September · Palma del Río → Córdoba

Medium mountain stage · 181 km · 3,130 m of climbing

Sierra Morena classic-style stage with three Cat3 climbs before the traditional reduced-group finish in Córdoba, on the eve of the second rest day.

  • Cat3Puerto de la Forestal — 15.5 km at 2.6%, summit 497 m, 146.5 km from the finish. Gradual climb into the Sierra Morena.
  • Cat3Alto de Villaviciosa de Córdoba — 11.59 km at 3.2%, summit 735 m, 59.5 km from the finish. Length/gradient calculated from Komoot's elevation chart between the base and summit markers.
  • Cat3Puerto Artafi — 6.28 km at 5.2%, summit 523 m, 27.8 km from the finish. Length/gradient calculated from Komoot's elevation chart for the correct (stage-direction) side — a different, steeper segment than CyclingCols' only listed profile, which climbs the opposite way from Medina Azahara.

Stage 16 · Tue 8 September · Cortegana → La Rábida. Palos de la Frontera

Hilly stage · 186 km · 1,980 m of climbing

One of the last chances for the sprinters — winding through Huelva's hills before a flat run along the coast to the Columbus monuments at La Rábida.

Rolling stage with 7 uncategorised climbs.

Stage 17 · Wed 9 September · Dos Hermanas → Sevilla

Flat stage · 189 km · 890 m of climbing

The flattest stage of the race; an early breakaway is the main variable before a bunch sprint through Seville's streets.

Stage 18 · Thu 10 September · El Puerto de Santa María → Jerez de la Frontera

Individual time trial · 32 km · 190 m of climbing

Second and final time trial, along the Cádiz coastline where the wind could make real time differences among the GC contenders.

Stage 19 · Fri 11 September · Vélez-Málaga → Peñas Blancas. Estepona

Hilly stage · 205 km · 4,190 m of climbing

Flat and coastal for most of the day before the Serranía de Ronda piles on the fatigue ahead of the final climb to Peñas Blancas (Cat1) — scene of Richard Carapaz's 2022 stage win.

  • Cat2Puerto de Las Abejas — 10.5 km at 4.9%, summit 887 m, 115.8 km from the finish.
  • Cat2Puerto del Viento — 13.8 km at 3.8%, summit 1,078 m, 101.5 km from the finish. Climbed from the east, from El Burgo. Length/gradient from CyclingCols. Max gradient 9.6%.
  • Cat1Peñas Blancas — 19 km at 6.7%, summit 1,264 m, stage finish. Scene of Richard Carapaz's stage win in 2022, extended a few km longer than the 2013 version. Starts almost at sea level in Estepona; several ramps over 14%.

Stage 20 · Sat 12 September · La Calahorra → Collado del Alguacil

Mountain stage · 186.8 km · 5,000 m of climbing

Route revised after a landslide closed the road to Alto de Hazallanas — the stage now climbs Puerto de Los Blancares, Puerto de El Purche twice, and the new Puerto de El Duque, before the same unprecedented summit finish at Collado del Alguacil — 8.3km at 9.8%.

  • Cat3Puerto de Los Blancares — 8.53 km at 3.75%, summit 1,298 m, 136.1 km from the finish. Unaffected by the stage-20 landslide reroute. Length/gradient from Komoot's elevation chart for the correct (Guadix-side) direction.
  • Cat1Puerto de El Purche (1st ascent) — 6.96 km at 9%, summit 1,483 m, 92.5 km from the finish. Climbed via Huétor Vega. Length/gradient from Komoot's GPS route data.
  • Cat1Puerto de El Purche (2nd ascent) — 6.96 km at 9%, summit 1,483 m, 61.3 km from the finish. Second ascent of the day, same climb as earlier.
  • Cat1Puerto de El Duque — 4.8 km at 8.2%, summit 1,670 m, 31.4 km from the finish. Never before climbed in Vuelta history. Ascends the same Genil River valley as Alto de Hazallanas, with an identical final 2km finishing on the same road to the Sierra Nevada ski resort — but shorter and slightly easier, and without the 18-19% ramps that mark Hazallanas' opening kilometres after the technical descent from Güéjar Sierra.
  • ESPCollado del Alguacil — 8.3 km at 9.8%, summit 1,884 m, stage finish. Unprecedented summit finish and La Vuelta 26's last great challenge — 8.3km at an official average of 9.8%.

Stage 21 · Sun 13 September · Carrefour Granada → Granada

Flat stage · 99 km · 1,280 m of climbing

Ceremonial-style final stage with a circuit finish past the Alhambra, crowning the winner of La Vuelta 26 in Granada.
